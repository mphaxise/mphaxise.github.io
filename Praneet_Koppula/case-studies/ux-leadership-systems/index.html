Précis

A growing distributed UX organization needed intentional systems for developing leaders and allocating limited capacity. I designed and operated planning, capability, coverage, development, and listening mechanisms with leadership peers; the work made leadership pathways, skill-gap choices, and resource tradeoffs more explicit.

The organization had to scale leadership, capability, and coverage at the same time.

Context and stakes

Informal structure stopped being enough.

The UX organization was distributed across product areas, roles, levels, and geographies. Product demand exceeded available design and research capacity. Newer practitioners needed onboarding and upskilling while experienced practitioners needed credible leadership pathways.

The management problem was systemic: how to build bench strength, identify capability gaps, plan coverage, support readiness decisions, listen to the team, and adapt without fragmenting the organization.

Role and collaboration

The work involved UX leaders, managers, team leads, practitioners, recruiting partners, and product leadership. I focus here on the operating system while keeping individual decisions confidential.

01 / BENCH

Map leadership capacity before drawing structure.

I evaluated where the organization needed stronger mentoring, onboarding, research and design support, product-area coverage, and continuity. The models considered interest, experience, readiness, growth timing, and the work a lead would actually need to do.

The aim was not to fill boxes on an org chart. It was to define a structure that could develop people while supporting the work.

02 / CAPABILITY

Connect role gaps to development choices.

A structured leadership session examined gaps in visual design, content, interaction design, UX strategy, and related capabilities. The group considered alternatives such as hiring, coaching, communities of practice, specialist support, and role development.

The value was the connection between capability and action. Each gap led to a decision about development, staffing, or operating support.

03 / COVERAGE

Treat capacity as a scenario, not a fixed ratio.

Coverage planning compared role mix, product demand, geography, onboarding cost, leadership capacity, and hiring constraints. Scenarios made tradeoffs visible without pretending that one ratio could describe every team.

Structured check-ins added a qualitative feedback layer. They helped test whether the planned structure was supporting people and where coaching or operating adjustments were needed.

People-first boundary: the model guides aggregate planning while individual readiness, staffing, and team-health decisions remain confidential.
Work in action

A management system with several feedback loops.

The operating rhythm linked organizational demand to leadership development and team health.

  1. Listen

    Gather product demand, manager input, practitioner feedback, and team-health signals.

  2. Map

    Describe capacity, role mix, capability gaps, leadership bench, and coverage pressure.

  3. Model

    Compare leadership, staffing, and geographic support scenarios.

  4. Develop

    Create lead pathways, ramp-up support, coaching, and capability plans.

  5. Review

    Evaluate readiness and operating health through structured management review.

  6. Adjust

    Change coaching, staffing, coverage, or structure as the organization evolves.

Outcomes

A more intentional infrastructure for UX leadership.

The clearest outcome was planning infrastructure that made leadership pathways, capability choices, and resource tradeoffs explicit.

Scaling UX is an organizational design problem.

Leadership bench, role clarity, capability, coverage, and team listening are interdependent. Optimizing one in isolation creates pressure elsewhere.

The durable lesson is to make the tradeoffs visible while protecting the dignity and privacy of the people inside the system.
