Précis

A broad product portfolio created more possible UX work than a specialist team could address project by project. I authored Target Workflow and ownership artifacts and contributed to the collaborative lexicon, Top-N model, and advisory work; the resulting operating model connected priorities, owners, evidence, roadmap actions, and review.

Workflow became the bridge between customer value and portfolio action.

Context and stakes

Feature-local UX could not reveal the whole journey.

Workflow knowledge was scattered across product teams, studies, release plans, quality signals, support evidence, and institutional memory. Teams also used different language for journeys, stages, tasks, steps, and variants.

Senior stakeholders needed a clear view of important current workflows and strategic future workflows. Product teams needed owners, evidence, and follow-through. UX leaders needed a defensible way to focus limited specialist capacity.

Role and collaboration

01 / LANGUAGE

Define a workflow language teams could share.

The method separated a user's role and goal from the workflow, its stages, tasks, steps, and variants. That structure was detailed enough to expose pain points without tying the definition to one interface or release.

It also made evidence attachable. Research, quality signals, customer requests, and benchmark data could remain tied to a specific part of a workflow.

02 / PRIORITY

Balance workflows of today and tomorrow.

The Top-N model separated high-frequency current workflows from strategic future workflows that could close a meaningful gap or create a new opportunity.

The criteria asked whether a workflow represented a real user goal, had enough public or internal reference material to inspect, aligned with product strategy, and justified coordinated attention.

Scope: the examples use generic workflow categories so the operating logic remains visible without disclosing product priorities or business rationale.
03 / OWNERSHIP

Connect ownership, evidence, and follow-through.

A workflow owner and core team could map the current state, add evidence layers, identify pain points, benchmark the experience, define roadmap actions, and revisit progress across releases.

The same structure improved advisory communication. Leaders could discuss customer work, evidence, ownership, and resource needs through one common frame that connected previously separate project updates.

Work in action

A repeatable portfolio cycle.

The operating model connected method, prioritization, delivery, and learning.

  1. Inventory

    Collect candidate workflows across product areas using one shared definition.

  2. Select

    Prioritize important current workflows and strategic future workflows.

  3. Assign

    Name a workflow owner and a cross-functional core team.

  4. Map

    Describe stages, tasks, steps, variants, pain points, and evidence.

  5. Act

    Translate the evidence into roadmap and release decisions.

  6. Review

    Communicate progress, revisit priorities, and refresh the inventory.

Outcomes

A stronger decision infrastructure for portfolio UX.

The clearest outcome was decision infrastructure: shared language, ownership, and a sharper way to direct specialist UX capacity.
