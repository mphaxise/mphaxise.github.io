The Easy Version of the Plan

This keeps the moving days reasonable and gives us two real rest/explore days before the final lake stop.

Wed Jul 15

Drive to Lassen

Set up at Manzanita Lake, eat a low-effort camp dinner, walk near the lake if we still have energy.

Thu Jul 16

Volcano day

Choose Manzanita Lake Loop or Devastated Area. If a ranger program is open, that becomes the evening bonus.

Fri Jul 17

Drive to redwoods

Leave after breakfast, take snack stops, arrive in the cooler forest, and keep the evening simple.

Sat Jul 18

Redwood explorer day

Pick one gentle grove walk, then add beach, visitor center, picnic, or hot chocolate depending on mood.

Sun Jul 19

Drive to Crater Lake

Arrive at Mazama, set up, then go see the rim. This is the big blue lake moment.

Mon Jul 20

Morning lake walk, then home

Do one short rim walk or waterfall trail, then start the long drive home before the trip gets too stretched.

Hike Ideas for a 9-Year-Old

None of these are meant to be a forced march. The goal is one good walk each day, plenty of snack pauses, and a few things to notice that make the place feel magical.

Manzanita Lake Loop

EasyAbout 1.8 milesLake views

A relaxed loop right from the campground area. Good for a first evening or a slow morning.

What we will notice: Lassen Peak reflected in the water, old volcanic rock around the lake, and how mountain forests stay cooler than the valley.

Devastated Area Trail

Very easyAbout 0.5 milesVolcano story

A short interpretive walk where the landscape explains the 1915 Lassen eruption in a kid-sized way.

What we will notice: Big rocks moved by the eruption, forest slowly growing back, and how a mountain can change a place without making it scary.

Stout Grove or Simpson-Reed

EasyUnder 1 mileGiant trees

A short grove walk for the classic redwood feeling. Pick whichever is easier from camp that day.

What we will notice: Bark that protects trees like armor, sunlight high above us, soft forest floor, and tree rings that count time.

Lady Bird Johnson Grove

EasyAbout 1.5 milesFog forest

A beautiful loop if we want a little more walking without making the day too hard.

What we will notice: Coast fog helping redwoods drink, tiny plants living on fallen logs, and the forest making its own cool air.

Discovery Point

Easy-moderateChoose 1-2 milesBlue lake

A rim walk where we can go as far as we feel like going and turn around whenever the view feels complete.

What we will notice: A collapsed volcano filled by rain and snow, Wizard Island, and why the lake looks so intensely blue.

Plaikni Falls

EasyAbout 2 milesWaterfall

A gentle forest trail to a waterfall, especially good if we want movement without cliff-edge drama.

What we will notice: Snowmelt becoming a stream, summer wildflowers, and how water shapes the mountain slowly.

Small Wonders to Look For

Instead of focusing on bugs or animals, we can turn the trip into a quiet treasure hunt for rocks, water, trees, colors, and night sky moments.

V

Volcano clues

At Lassen, we can look for rocks that tell stories: lava, ash, big eruption boulders, and mountains that changed shape.

T

Trees that make weather

In the redwoods, fog, shade, and huge trunks make a cooler little world. We can compare it to the sunny drive.

B

Blue-water science

At Crater Lake, the water is deep, clear, and mostly filled by rain and snow. That is why the blue feels unreal.

S

Stars and quiet

The simplest plan may be the best one: warm layers, one headlamp walk near camp, and looking for the first bright star.
